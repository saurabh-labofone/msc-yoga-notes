Each system of medicine says what health is. Yoga adds one idea: sickness often starts in the mind (Adhi) and then reaches the body (Vyadhi).

    What is health? The WHO answer

    SyllabusConcept of Health According to WHO

    Health is full well-being of body, mind and social life — not only "no disease".

    Think of it like thisA plant with no disease is not yet a happy plant. It must also grow, flower and live well with the plants around it. Health is more than "not sick".
    Remember
    1. Source: the WHO Constitution, 1948.
    2. Health is positive. No disease is not enough.
    3. Three sides: body, mind, social life. In 1984 the spiritual side was added.
    4. Yoga adds the idea of balance (Samatva).
    For the exam — what to write
    "A state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity." — WHO Constitution, 1948

    The four sides (dimensions)

    • Physical · mental · social — in the WHO definition.
    • Spiritual — in 1984 the World Health Assembly asked countries to add it to health plans.
    • Yoga works on all four.

    Mental health (WHO)

    A state of mental well-being in which a person can cope with normal stress, use their abilities, learn and work well, and help their community.

    Exam tip

    Write the WHO definition word for word. Then say what yoga adds: the spiritual side and the idea of balance (Samatva).

    Answer plan — health, and yoga's role in prevention and cure

    1. WHO definition word for word + the 4 sides.
    2. Sushruta's Svastha verse + its 5 parts. Charaka's two aims (Point 2).
    3. Yoga's view: Samatva, Chitta Prasadana, Heyam duhkham anagatam (Point 5).
    4. Prevention: daily practice stops disease before it starts. Cure: Adhija Vyadhi, IAYT.
    5. One research line from Unit 4.

    Health in India's old medical systems — Ayurveda, Naturopathy, Siddha

    SyllabusConcept of Health According to … Indian Systems of Medicines i.e. Ayurveda, Naturopathy and Siddha Systems of Medicine

    All three systems say health is balance inside the body and mind.

    Think of it like thisA see-saw. When both sides are even, it is steady. Each system names what must stay even in you.
    SystemHealth means
    AyurvedaBalanced doshas, digestive fire, tissues and waste — and a happy self, senses and mind
    NaturopathyThe body's vital force flows freely. No waste (toxins) is stored.
    SiddhaBalance of 3 humours: Vatham, Pitham, Kabam
    Remember
    1. Ayurveda's healthy person = Svastha (Sushruta Samhita).
    2. Ayurveda has two aims: keep the healthy well and cure the sick (Charaka Samhita).
    3. Naturopathy heals with the 5 elements. Gandhi promoted it.
    4. Siddha comes from Tamil Nadu and the 18 Siddhars.
    For the exam — what to write

    Ayurveda

    samadoṣaḥ samāgniśca samadhātumalakriyaḥ · prasannātmendriyamanāḥ svastha ityabhidhīyate — Sushruta Samhita, Sutra Sthana 15

    A healthy person (Svastha) has 5 things: balanced doshas (Vata, Pitta, Kapha) · balanced digestive fire (Agni) · balanced 7 tissues (Dhatus) · normal removal of waste (Malas) · a pleased self, senses and mind (Prasanna Atma, Indriya, Manas).

    The two aims of Ayurveda (Charaka Samhita): protect the health of the healthy (Svasthasya svasthya rakshanam) and cure the disease of the sick (Aturasya vikara prashamanam).

    Naturopathy

    • The body heals itself through its vital force. The doctor only removes what blocks it.
    • Disease = the body collects waste (toxins, morbid matter). All diseases have one cause and one cure ("unity of disease").
    • Treatment uses the five elements: earth (mud therapy), water (hydrotherapy), fire (sun bath), air (fresh air, breathing), space (fasting).
    • Mahatma Gandhi promoted Nature Cure in India.

    Siddha

    • The old medical system of Tamil Nadu, from the 18 Siddhars. Agastya is the first.
    • Health = balance of three humours: Vatham, Pitham, Kabam, in the ratio 1 : ½ : ¼.
    • Medicines from herbs, minerals and metals.
    • Diagnosis by pulse (Naadi) and 8 methods (Envagai Thervu).
    • Principle: food is medicine and medicine is food.

    All five side by side

    SystemHealth isDisease starts inMain tools
    WHOComplete physical, mental, social well-beingMany causesMedicine, public health
    AyurvedaBalance of dosha, agni, dhatu, mala + happy mindDosha imbalance, weak agniHerbs, diet, Panchakarma
    NaturopathyFree flow of vital force, no toxinsToxins in the body5 elements, fasting, diet
    SiddhaBalance of Vatham, Pitham, KabamHumour imbalanceHerbo-mineral medicine
    YogaBalance (Samatva) and a calm mind (Chitta Prasadana)The mind (Adhi) → body (Vyadhi)Asana, pranayama, meditation, ethics

    What these systems do well, and where they fall short

    SyllabusUtility and Limitations of these systems in health and healing

    They are strong for prevention and long illness, but weak for emergencies.

    Think of it like thisGood food keeps you strong for years. But if you break a leg, you need a hospital, not a diet. Use each tool for its own job.
    Remember
    1. Good at: whole-person care, prevention, lifestyle diseases, low cost.
    2. Weak at: emergencies, accidents, sudden infections, surgery.
    3. Fewer large trials (RCTs). Medicines are not always standard.
    4. Best together with modern medicine, not in place of it.
    For the exam — what to write

    Utility — what they do well

    • Holistic: treat body, mind and lifestyle together.
    • Strong in prevention and lifestyle diseases (diabetes, BP, obesity, stress).
    • Low cost, easy to reach in villages.
    • Fewer side effects when done correctly.
    • Teach self-care, so the patient takes part in healing.
    • Good support next to modern medicine (integrative care).

    Limitations — where they fall short

    • Slow. Not for emergencies, accidents, acute infections or surgery.
    • Fewer large, high-quality trials (RCTs).
    • Medicines are not always standard. Some products had heavy-metal problems.
    • Need trained, qualified practitioners. Self-treatment can be harmful.
    • Results depend on the patient's regular practice and discipline.
    • Diagnosis methods (pulse, dosha) are hard to measure objectively.

    Exam tip

    End with balance. These systems work best together with modern medicine, not in place of it. The syllabus itself says yoga therapists work "under the guidance of qualified medical specialists".

    Answer plan

    1. Introduce Ayurveda, Naturopathy and Siddha in 2 lines each + the side-by-side table (Point 2).
    2. Utility list.
    3. Limitations list.
    4. End with integrative care.

    Mind trouble becomes body trouble — Adhi and Vyadhi

    SyllabusYogic Concept of Health and Disease: Concept of Adhi and Vyadhi; Concept of stress (Adhi) as cause for Vyadhi (yoga vasishta)

    Stress in the mind (Adhi) can turn into disease of the body (Vyadhi).

    Think of it like thisWorry before an exam can give you a stomach ache. The trouble starts in the mind. Then the body feels it. The Yoga Vasistha says many body diseases start this way.
    Adhi — stress in the mind (desire, anger, worry, likes and dislikes)
    ↓
    The mind is shaken (Chitta Vikshepa)
    ↓
    The prana is shaken (Prana Kshobha) — breath becomes fast and uneven; prana flows the wrong way in the Nadis
    ↓
    Digestion goes wrong — food is digested badly (Ajirna), too fast (Atijirna) or wrongly (Kujirna)
    ↓
    Vyadhi — disease of the body (asthma, high BP, ulcer, diabetes, IBS)
    Remember
    1. Adhi = disease of the mind. Vyadhi = disease of the body.
    2. Source: the Yoga Vasistha (Nirvana Prakarana).
    3. Body disease born from Adhi = Adhija Vyadhi (mind-made, psychosomatic).
    4. Remove the stress, and that disease goes.
    For the exam — what to write

    The 4 kinds

    TypeMeaningCure
    Samanya Adhi (ordinary)Day-to-day stressGood conduct, yoga practice, mantra
    Sara Adhi (essential)The deep bondage of birth and deathOnly self-knowledge (Jnana)
    Adhija VyadhiBody disease born from Adhi = psychosomaticRemove the stress → the disease goes
    Anadhija VyadhiBody disease not from the mind: infection, injuryMedicine, surgery, right food

    Stress in modern science

    • Hans Selye: stress is the body's response to any demand.
    • General Adaptation Syndrome has 3 stages: alarm → resistance → exhaustion.
    • Good stress = eustress. Harmful stress = distress.
    • Long stress keeps the stress-hormone line (HPA axis) and the alarm nerves (sympathetic system) switched on → high cortisol, high BP, weak immunity.

    Answer plan

    1. Define Adhi and Vyadhi. Name the Yoga Vasistha.
    2. Draw the 5-step flow.
    3. Samanya vs Sara Adhi.
    4. Adhija vs Anadhija Vyadhi.
    5. Selye's 3 stages as the modern link.

    Stop the pain before it comes — Heyam duhkham anagatam

    SyllabusRole of Yoga in preventive health care – Heyam dukham anagatam

    Pain that has not come yet can be avoided (Yoga Sutra 2.16).

    Think of it like thisYou put on a raincoat before the rain, not after. Yoga works the same way: it keeps sickness away before it starts.
    Remember
    1. Past pain is over. Present pain we bear. Future pain we can stop.
    2. The cause (YS 2.17): the self takes itself to be the body and mind.
    3. 3 levels of prevention: primary (stop it before it starts), secondary (find it early), tertiary (stop it getting worse).
    4. Yoga is strongest at the primary level.
    For the exam — what to write
    heyaṃ duḥkham anāgatam — Yoga Sutra 2.16

    "The pain that has not yet come can be avoided." Past pain is over. Present pain we must bear. Future pain we can stop.

    The cause — YS 2.17

    The joining of the seer and the seen (Drashtr–Drishya Samyoga): the self takes itself to be the body and mind. Yoga removes this wrong identity, so the cause of future pain goes.

    3 levels of prevention

    • Primary: stop disease before it starts.
    • Secondary: find and treat it early.
    • Tertiary: stop it getting worse.

    Yoga is strongest at the primary level: daily asana, pranayama, diet and a calm mind.

    Answer plan

    1. YS 2.16 + meaning.
    2. YS 2.17: the cause.
    3. The 3 levels of prevention.
    4. Yoga at the primary level, with examples.

    The 5 troubles of the mind (Kleshas) — root causes of illness

    SyllabusPotential causes of Ill-health: Kleshas

    Five troubles in the mind push us into habits that make us ill.

    Think of it like thisA tree. Not-knowing (Avidya) is the soil. The "I am the body" feeling grows from it. Then three branches grow: liking, disliking and fear. Illness is the bad fruit.
    Remember
    1. Avidya — not-knowing. Asmita — "I am the body and mind".
    2. Raga — liking → overeating, addiction.
    3. Dvesha — disliking, anger → high BP, heart disease.
    4. Abhinivesha — fear of death → anxiety, panic.
    5. Kriya Yoga makes them thin. Meditation removes their active form.
    For the exam — what to write
    avidyāsmitārāgadveṣābhiniveśāḥ kleśāḥ — Yoga Sutra 2.3
    KleshaMeaningHow it makes us ill
    AvidyaIgnorance: taking the passing for the lasting, the impure for pure, pain for pleasure, non-self for self (YS 2.5)The root of all wrong choices
    Asmita"I am the body and mind" (YS 2.6)Hurt ego → stress, pride, comparison
    RagaPull towards pleasure (YS 2.7)Overeating, addiction, obesity, diabetes
    DveshaPush away from pain (YS 2.8)Anger, hate → high BP, heart disease, ulcer
    AbhiniveshaClinging to life, fear of death — even in the wise (YS 2.9)Anxiety, phobia, panic

    4 states of a Klesha — YS 2.4

    Asleep (Prasupta) · thin (Tanu) · interrupted (Vicchinna) · fully active (Udara).

    How to remove them

    1. Kriya Yoga — Tapas, Svadhyaya, Ishvara Pranidhana — makes them thin (YS 2.1–2.2).
    2. Meditation (Dhyana) removes their active forms (YS 2.11).
    3. The subtle seeds dissolve when the mind goes back to its source (Pratiprasava, YS 2.10).

      Energy channels and centres (Nadis and Chakras)

      SyllabusConcepts of … nadis, chakras, and their role in health and healing

      Nadis are channels that carry prana. Chakras are centres where many nadis meet.

      Think of it like thisNadis are like water pipes in a house. Chakras are the places where many pipes join. If a pipe is blocked, water cannot flow. If a nadi is blocked, prana cannot flow.
      Remember
      1. 72,000 nadis (HYP 4.18). Three main ones: Ida, Pingala, Sushumna.
      2. Ida = left, moon, cooling. Pingala = right, sun, heating.
      3. Blocked nadis → prana cannot flow → disease. Nadi Shodhana cleans them.
      4. 7 chakras, from Muladhara (base of spine) to Sahasrara (crown).
      For the exam — what to write

      The 3 main nadis

      • Ida — left side · moon · cooling · calm. Often compared with the parasympathetic (rest) system.
      • Pingala — right side · sun · heating · active. Often compared with the sympathetic (alarm) system.
      • Sushumna — centre of the spine · the spiritual channel. It opens when Ida and Pingala are balanced.

      Careful: Ida/Pingala = parasympathetic/sympathetic is a comparison scholars use, not a proven match. Write "is compared with" in the exam.

      Why nadis matter for health

      Blocked or impure nadis → prana cannot flow → disease. Nadi Shodhana pranayama cleans them. The Hatha Yoga Pradipika (2.19–20) lists the signs of pure nadis: a light, slim body, a bright face, clear inner sound, good digestion.

      • Raghuraj & Telles 2008: right-nostril breathing raised BP. Alternate-nostril breathing lowered it.

      The 6 cleaning steps (Shuddhi Prakriyas)

        What and how much to eat — Hatha Yoga and the Gita

        SyllabusDietary regulation according to Hatha yoga and Bhagavadgita

        Eat moderate, pleasant food, and leave a quarter of the stomach empty.

        Think of it like thisA pot of rice needs room to boil. Fill it to the top and it spills over. The stomach also needs empty space to digest.
        Remember
        1. Mitahara = moderate diet (HYP 1.58).
        2. Overeating is first of six things that destroy yoga (HYP 1.15).
        3. Gita 6.16–17: be moderate (Yukta) in food, play, work, sleep and waking.
        4. Gita foods: Sattvic gives health, Rajasic gives pain, Tamasic gives dullness.
        For the exam — what to write

        Hatha Yoga — Mitahara (HYP 1.58)

        Food that is pleasant and mild, eaten with a quarter of the stomach left empty, and offered to God before eating. The split ½ food, ¼ water, ¼ empty comes from the Gheranda Samhita (ch. 5).

        Pathya — good for a yogi (HYP 1.62–63)

        Wheat, rice, barley, milk, ghee, butter, honey, dry ginger, green gram (mung), light vegetables, pure water. Food that is nourishing, sweet, and suits the body.

        Apathya — avoid (HYP 1.59–61)

        Very bitter, sour, salty, hot or pungent food · fermented and stale food · re-heated food · too much oil · alcohol, meat, fish · onion and garlic (in strict practice) · overeating.

        HYP 1.15 lists overeating (Atyahara) first among six things that destroy yoga.

        Bhagavad Gita

        Gita 6.16–17: yoga is not for one who eats too much or too little, or sleeps too much or too little. It is for one who is moderate (Yukta) in food, play, work, sleep and waking.

        FoodWhat it is likeWhat it does
        Sattvic (17.8)Juicy, smooth, substantial, pleasingIncreases life, purity, strength, health, happiness
        Rajasic (17.9)Too bitter, sour, salty, hot, pungent, dry, burningCauses pain, grief and disease
        Tamasic (17.10)Stale, tasteless, rotten, left-over, impureCauses dullness

          Unit 4 · Point 5 of 6Must learn

          What yoga does in a healthy body (healthy volunteers)

          SyllabusLiterature on psycho-physiological research in normal healthy volunteers

          In healthy people, yoga practices change breath, heart and brain in ways we can measure.

          Think of it like thisA new shoe is first tested on healthy runners. You learn what it does before you give it to injured people. Healthy-volunteer studies do the same for yoga.
          Remember
          1. Telles 1994: right-nostril breathing raised oxygen use by 37%.
          2. Raghuraj & Telles 2008: alternate-nostril breathing lowered BP. Right-nostril breathing raised it.
          3. Streeter 2007: brain GABA (a calming chemical) rose 27% after one session.
          4. Ross & Thomas 2010: yoga was equal to or better than exercise, except for fitness.
