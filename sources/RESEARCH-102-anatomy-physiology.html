Research papers for Paper 102 — Anatomy & Physiology

Checked on PubMed on 6 Nov 2026. Findings come from the abstracts only. Open any paper at https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/<PMID>/.

Unit 1 — cells, tissues, homeostasis, nerve potential

Paper PMID Finding
Tolahunase, Sagar, Dada 2017, Oxid Med Cell Longev 28191278 12 weeks yoga + meditation (n = 96, no control): ROS, DNA-damage marker 8-OH2dG, cortisol and IL-6 fell. Telomerase and BDNF rose. Telomere length change not significant (p = 0.069).
Lavretsky et al. 2013, Int J Geriatr Psychiatry 22407663 RCT, n = 39 caregivers: Kirtan Kriya 12 min/day for 8 weeks raised telomerase 43% vs 3.7% with relaxing music.
Bhasin et al. 2013, PLoS One 23650531 Relaxation-response practice raised genes for mitochondrial energy and telomere upkeep, lowered NF-κB inflammation genes. Not yoga alone.
Pascoe, Thompson, Ski 2017, Psychoneuroendocrinology 28963884 Meta-analysis, 42 RCTs: yoga with asanas lowered cortisol, ambulatory systolic BP, resting HR, fasting glucose, cholesterol, LDL.
Malhotra et al. 2002, Indian J Physiol Pharmacol 12613392 40 days of asanas (n = 20 + 20): median nerve conduction velocity rose in type 2 diabetes.
Telles, Chetry, Balkrishna 2024, Int J Yoga 39444668 n = 22: auditory evoked potential (Pa wave) amplitude rose during high-frequency yoga breathing.

Unit 2 — digestion and breathing

Paper PMID Finding
Abel, Lloyd, Williams 2013, J Altern Complement Med 22978245 Review, 9 studies: yoga improved MIP, MEP, MVV, FVC, FEV1, PEFR in 8 of 9. Needed ≥ 10 weeks.
Mooventhan, Khode 2014, Int J Yoga 25035619 RCT, n = 82: 2 weeks Bhramari + OM raised PEF, FEF25%, MVV. FVC unchanged.
Telles, Singh, Balkrishna 2015, Med Sci Monit Basic Res 26270968 High-frequency yoga breathing (1.0 Hz) raised ventilation, VO2, VCO2. VO2 returned to baseline after.
Bernardi et al. 1998, Lancet 9643792 Heart failure: training to 6 breaths/min raised resting SaO2 from 92.5% to 93.2%.
Liu et al. 2014, J Thorac Dis 24977005 Meta-analysis, 5 RCTs, n = 233 COPD: FEV1 +123.57 mL, 6-min walk +38.84 m. PaO2, PaCO2 unchanged.
Schumann et al. 2016, Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol 27112106 Review, 6 RCTs, n = 273: yoga reduced IBS symptoms, severity and anxiety.
Panigrahi et al. 2024, Indian J Gastroenterol 38112914 n = 238: Shankhaprakshalana gave good bowel cleansing (BBPS 8 ± 1.2), minor side effects 4.2%.

Unit 3 — heart, vessels, blood

Paper PMID Finding
Bernardi et al. 2001, BMJ 11751348 n = 23: mantra or rosary slowed breathing to 6/min. Baroreflex sensitivity 9.5 → 11.5 ms/mmHg.
Pal, Velkumary, Madanmohan 2004, Indian J Med Res 15347862 RCT, n = 60, 3 months: slow breathing raised parasympathetic and lowered sympathetic activity. Fast breathing did not.
Joseph et al. 2005, Hypertension 16129818 6 breaths/min in hypertension: BP 149.7/82.7 → 141.1/77.8 mmHg. Baroreflex 5.8 → 10.3 ms/mmHg.
Hagins et al. 2013, Evid Based Complement Alternat Med 23781266 Meta-analysis, 17 studies: BP −4.17/−3.62 mmHg. Full practice (asana + breath + meditation) −8.17/−6.14.
Cramer et al. 2014, Am J Hypertens 24795403 Meta-analysis, 7 RCTs, n = 452: vs usual care −9.65/−7.22 mmHg. Very low-quality evidence. No better than exercise.
Telles et al. 2013, Med Sci Monit 23334063 RCT, n = 90 hypertension: 10 min Anulom Vilom lowered systolic (p < 0.001) and diastolic (p < 0.05) BP.
Chohan et al. 1984, Thromb Haemost 6740553 n = 7, 4 months: fibrinolytic activity rose, fibrinogen fell, Hb and haematocrit rose. Very small study.

Unit 4 — bones, joints, muscles

Paper PMID Finding
Tran et al. 2001, Prev Cardiol 11832673 n = 10, 8 weeks hatha yoga: strength +19–31%, knee-flexion endurance +57%, trunk-extension flexibility +188%.
Sivaramakrishnan et al. 2019, Int J Behav Nutr Phys Act 30953508 Meta-analysis, 22 RCTs, older adults: yoga improved balance, flexibility and leg strength.
Lu, Rosner, Chang, Fishman 2016, Top Geriatr Rehabil 27226695 12-min daily routine, no control: small spine BMD gain (P = .005), femur borderline (P = .053).
Fernández-Rodríguez et al. 2021, PLoS One 33961670 Meta-analysis, 11 studies, n = 591: no significant BMD effect (ES 0.07). Read as "maintained".
Lauche et al. 2019, Curr Rheumatol Rep 31338685 Meta-analysis, 9 RCTs, n = 640: knee OA pain, stiffness and function improved. Very low quality.
Wieland et al. 2022, Cochrane 36398843 21 RCTs, n = 2223: small gains in back pain and function, no better than other back exercise.
Telles et al. 2016, Med Sci Monit 27619104 RCT, low back pain: yoga reduced pain and anxiety. MRI disc changes did not change.

How to cite in the exam

Write first author + year + one finding. Example: "Bernardi et al. (2001) showed that breathing at 6 per minute raises baroreflex sensitivity." State weak designs honestly when you use them (small n, no control group).
