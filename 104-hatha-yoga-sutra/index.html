Cleansing and body practices

Hatha Yoga works from the outside in. Clean the body, make it steady, control the breath, then seal the energy.

Syllabus title: Foundational & Cleansing Practices for Physical and Energetic Health

    Unit 1 · Point 1 of 4Must learn

    The 6 cleansing practices (Shatkarma)

    SyllabusShatkarma (Cleansing Techniques): Concept and purpose of internal purification, Physiological rationale behind Neti, Dhauti, Basti, Nauli, Kapalabhati, and Trataka … Contraindications and precautions

    Shatkarma is six ways to clean the body before the bigger practices.

    Think of it like thisBefore you cook, you wash the pot. Shatkarma washes the body first. Then asana and pranayama work better.
    PracticeWhat it cleans
    NetiThe nose
    DhautiThe stomach
    BastiThe large intestine (colon)
    NauliMassages the belly organs
    KapalabhatiThe airways
    TratakaThe eyes, and trains attention
    Remember
    1. HYP 2.22 names the six: Dhauti, Basti, Neti, Trataka, Nauli, Kapalabhati.
    2. HYP 2.21: a person with too much fat or mucus must do them before pranayama.
    3. 5 jobs: remove waste, guard health (immunity), help digestion, prevent disease, wake the mind.
    4. Learn from a trained teacher, on an empty stomach.
    For the exam — what to write
    dhautirbastistathā netistrāṭakaṃ naulikaṃ tathā · kapālabhātiścaitāni ṣaṭkarmāṇi pracakṣate — Hatha Yoga Pradipika 2.22

    Who needs it — HYP 2.21

    Clean the body first, so the later practices work. A person with too much fat or mucus (Meda, Shleshma) must do Shatkarma before pranayama. A person whose three doshas are balanced does not need it.

    The 6 practices — method and body reason

    PracticeMethod in one lineWhy it worksUse
    Neti
    Jala, Sutra    		Lukewarm salt water (or a soft thread) goes in one nostril and out the otherWashes out mucus, dust and allergens. Helps the nose's own cleaning hairs (mucociliary clearance).Sinusitis, allergy, cold, headache
    Dhauti
    Vaman (Kunjal), Vastra, Danda    		Drink salt water and vomit it out, or swallow a cloth stripRemoves stomach mucus and extra acid. Stimulates the vagus nerve.Acidity, indigestion, mucus, asthma
    BastiWater is drawn into the large intestine, then pushed outCleans the colon. Removes gas and old stool.Constipation, gas
    NauliIsolate and roll the central belly muscles (rectus abdominis)Massages belly organs. Raises gut movement (peristalsis). Changes belly pressure.Digestion, constipation
    KapalabhatiFast, forceful out-breaths. The in-breath is passive.Active belly-muscle out-breath. Clears the airways. Wakes up the nervous system (sympathetic).Mucus, obesity, dullness
    TratakaGaze at a candle flame without blinking until tears comeTears clean the eyes. Trains one-pointed attention.Concentration, sleep, eye strain

    Role in health

    • Detoxification: removes mucus, extra acid and waste.
    • Immunity: clean airways and gut are the first line of defence.
    • Digestion: Dhauti, Nauli, Basti, Agnisara.
    • Prevention: keeps the doshas balanced.
    • Mind: Trataka and Kapalabhati make the mind alert.

    Do not do it if (contraindications)

    • High BP, heart disease → no Kapalabhati, Kunjal, Nauli.
    • Hernia, ulcer, recent surgery → no Nauli, Kunjal.
    • Pregnancy, menstruation → avoid strong kriyas.
    • Glaucoma, epilepsy → take care with Trataka.
    • Nose bleed, ear infection → no Neti.
    • Always learn from a trained teacher, on an empty stomach.

    Honest research lines

    • Swathi 2021 (a review of shatkarma studies): direct trials of shatkarma are still few.
    • Head 2018 (Cochrane, 14 studies, 747 people): salt-water nose rinsing — the principle of Jala Neti — eased allergy symptoms (SMD −1.32). Evidence quality is low. This is the best evidence for any kriya.
    • Safety — Yoder 2012: two people died from an amoeba in tap water used for nose rinsing. Use boiled, distilled or filtered water for Neti.

    Answer plan — "Need of Shatkarma"

    1. HYP 2.21: who needs it.
    2. The 6 practices table.
    3. The 5 roles in health.
    4. The contraindications.
    5. The honest research lines.

    Postures (Asana) — 3 types and their uses

    SyllabusAsana (Postures): Classification of poses: cultural, meditative, therapeutic, Role of asanas in posture correction, musculoskeletal health, rehabilitation, chronic pain management, Concept of sthirasukhamasanam and mind–body integration

    An asana is a posture that is steady and comfortable (YS 2.46).

    Think of it like thisYou sit in a good chair for a long talk. You do not fidget, and nothing hurts. That is steady and comfortable.
    TypeForExamples
    CulturalBody fitness: stretch, bend and twist the spineBhujangasana, Dhanurasana, Halasana
    MeditativeLong sitting with a straight spine and a still bodyPadmasana, Siddhasana, Vajrasana
    Relaxing / therapeuticRest, and special health problemsShavasana, Makarasana, Balasana
    Remember
    1. Steady (Sthira) + comfortable (Sukha) = asana (YS 2.46).
    2. 3 types: cultural, meditative, relaxing / therapeutic.
    3. HYP 1.17: asana gives steadiness, freedom from disease, a light body.
    4. 4 uses: better posture, muscles and joints, rehabilitation, chronic pain.
    For the exam — what to write
    sthirasukhamāsanam — Yoga Sutra 2.46

    Mind–body integration

    • YS 2.47: asana is perfected by relaxing effort (Prayatna Shaithilya) and by fixing the mind on the infinite (Ananta Samapatti).
    • YS 2.48: then the pairs of opposites — heat and cold, pleasure and pain — do not disturb you. This is mind–body integration.
    • HYP 1.17: asana gives steadiness (Sthairya), freedom from disease (Arogya) and lightness of the body (Anga Laghava).

    The 3 types — full examples

    • Cultural: Bhujangasana, Dhanurasana, Halasana, Sarvangasana, Paschimottanasana, Ardha Matsyendrasana.
    • Meditative: Padmasana, Siddhasana, Swastikasana, Sukhasana, Vajrasana.
    • Relaxing / therapeutic: Shavasana, Makarasana, Balasana. Vajrasana after meals. Tadasana for posture.

    Uses of asana

    UseHow asana helpsExample
    Posture correctionStrengthens weak back muscles. Stretches tight chest and hip muscles.Tadasana, Bhujangasana, Ushtrasana
    Muscles, bones, jointsFlexibility, joint range, muscle strength, loading of the bonesSurya Namaskar, standing poses
    RehabilitationSlow, controlled, step-by-step movement. Props help weak people.Lying-down and chair versions
    Chronic painMovement + relaxation + less fear of pain. Body awareness.Low back pain: Makarasana, Bhujangasana, Pavanamuktasana

    Asana and diet for strength, stamina, tone, flexibility

    • Strength and stamina: standing poses and Surya Namaskar.
    • Tone: holding the poses.
    • Flexibility: forward bends and back bends.
    • Diet: right amount (Mitahara) and Sattvic food (HYP 1.58–63).

    Short answer plan: the 3 types + YS 2.46–2.48 + HYP 1.17.

    Unit 1 · Point 3 of 4Must learn

    Breath control (Pranayama) — 4 kinds, and how it works

    SyllabusPranayama (Breath Regulation): Concept of Prana, Nadis, and the Pranic body, Balancing, cooling, heating, and tranquilizing pranayamas, Mechanisms influencing autonomic nervous system … Role in anxiety, stress, sleep disorders and immune strengthening

    Slow breath calms the body and mind. Fast breath wakes them up.

    Think of it like thisThe breath is like the brake and the accelerator of a car. Slow breath is the brake. Fast breath is the accelerator.
    Remember
    1. Prana = life energy. It flows in 72,000 channels (Nadis). The main three: Ida, Pingala, Sushumna.
    2. HYP 2.2: when the breath moves, the mind moves. When the breath is still, the mind is still.
    3. Slow breath, about 6 a minute, calms the heart and the mind.
    4. Fast breath (Bhastrika, Kapalabhati) wakes you up for a short time.
    For the exam — what to write

    The basics

    • Prana and the Nadis together make the Pranic body (Pranamaya Kosha).
    • YS 2.49: pranayama is the break (Viccheda) in the flow of in-breath and out-breath, after asana is mastered.
    • HYP 2.44 names 8 breath-holdings (Kumbhakas): Surya Bhedana, Ujjayi, Sitkari, Sitali, Bhastrika, Bhramari, Murchha, Plavini.
    • Swami Ramdev's daily set (Pranayam Rahasya, a syllabus book): Bhastrika, Kapalabhati, Bahya, Anulom-Vilom, Bhramari, Udgeeth, Pranava.

    The 4 kinds by effect

    KindPracticeEffect
    BalancingNadi Shodhana / Anulom-Vilom — alternate nostrilsBalances Ida and Pingala. Calms the mind.
    CoolingSheetali, Sheetkari, Chandra Bhedana — breathe in through the tongue, the teeth or the left nostrilLowers body heat, thirst, anger, BP
    HeatingSurya Bhedana, Bhastrika (and Kapalabhati) — right nostril, or fast strong breathRaises heat, energy, digestion. Not for high BP.
    TranquilizingBhramari, Ujjayi, Udgeeth (Om chanting) — long, slow out-breath with soundCalms anxiety. Helps sleep.

    How it works — the mechanism

    Slow breath, about 6 breaths a minute, with a long out-breath
    ↓
    Vagus nerve more active (parasympathetic ↑) · the blood-pressure reflex (baroreflex) more sensitive
    ↓
    Heart-rate variability ↑ · heart rate ↓ · blood pressure ↓
    ↓
    Calm emotions · less stress hormone (cortisol) · better sleep

    Fast breathing (Bhastrika, Kapalabhati) does the opposite for a short time: the sympathetic system goes up, and alertness goes up.

    Role in anxiety, stress, sleep, immunity

    • Anxiety and sleep: the tranquilizing kinds, with a long out-breath.
    • Stress: slow pranayama lowers heart rate and BP (Sharma 2013, below).
    • Immunity: [⚠️ PLACEHOLDER: a sourced line on pranayama and immune strength — not in the notes yet]

    Research

    • Bernardi 2001 (BMJ, n = 23): a mantra or the rosary slowed breathing to 6 a minute. Baroreflex sensitivity rose from 9.5 to 11.5 ms/mmHg.
    • Telles 2014 (Patanjali Research Foundation): during alternate-nostril breathing, systolic BP fell and vagal heart-rate variability (RMSSD, NN50) rose.
    • Sharma 2013 (RCT, 90 students, 12 weeks): fast and slow pranayama both cut perceived stress. Only slow pranayama (with Nadi Shodhana) lowered heart rate and diastolic BP.
    • Heating vs balancing: Telles 1994 (48 men): right-nostril breathing raised oxygen use by 37%. Raghuraj and Telles 2008: right-nostril breathing raised BP. Alternate-nostril breathing lowered it.
    • Tranquilizing: Yadav 2024 (RCT, 110 children with asthma): Bhramari and Om chanting gave asthma control in 68.2% vs 38.5%.

    Long answer — pranayama, mudra and bandha in purifying the Nadis

    1. Prana, the Nadis, the three main Nadis.
    2. Why Nadis get blocked. HYP 2.4–5: prana does not enter Sushumna while the Nadis are impure.
    3. Nadi Shodhana method (HYP 2.6–10). Signs of pure Nadis (HYP 2.19–20): light slim body, bright face, clear inner sound (Nada), good digestion.
    4. Bandhas and mudras hold the prana in the purified Nadis and direct it (Unit 1 · Point 4).
    5. The mechanism (vagus, HRV) + one study.

    Pranayama for lung capacity

    Deep, slow breathing trains the breathing muscles (diaphragm, intercostals). Kapalabhati and Bhastrika train the forceful out-breath. Measures: vital capacity, FEV1, peak flow (link to Paper 102, respiration).

    Dinesh 2015 (RCT, n = 91, 12 weeks): peak expiratory flow (PEFR) and FEF25–75 improved. FVC and FEV1 did not change significantly. Write this honestly.

    Short answer: classify pranayama by effect → draw the 2 × 2 box. How it acts on the nervous system → draw the mechanism flow.

    Seals and locks (Mudra and Bandha)

    SyllabusMudra (Energy Seals): Neuro-pranic mechanisms of Hasta Mudra, Kaya Mudra, Adhara Mudra (Pelvic Gestures), Lock Gestures, Effects on brain–body communication, concentration, pranic flow, and meditation readiness, Application in stress reduction and enhancing vitality

    A mudra sends prana where you want it. A bandha locks prana in one place.

    Think of it like thisA mudra is like pointing a garden hose: the water goes where you aim. A bandha is like closing a tap: the water stays in.
    GroupUsesExample
    Hand (Hasta)Finger positionsJnana / Chin Mudra
    Head (Mana)Eyes, tongue, ears, noseShambhavi, Shanmukhi
    Body (Kaya)Whole body, with breathViparita Karani
    Pelvic (Adhara)Pelvic-floor controlAshwini
    Lock (Bandha)Muscle locksMula, Uddiyana, Jalandhara
    Remember
    1. 5 groups: hand, head, body, pelvic, lock.
    2. 3 locks: throat (Jalandhara), belly (Uddiyana), root (Mula). All three together = Maha Bandha.
    3. HYP chapter 3 lists 10 mudras. The Gheranda Samhita gives 25.
    4. How it may work: the hands have a big area in the brain. The locks change pressure in the neck, belly and pelvis.
    For the exam — what to write

    The 10 mudras of the HYP (3.6–7)

    Mahamudra, Mahabandha, Mahavedha, Khechari, Uddiyana, Mula Bandha, Jalandhara, Viparita Karani, Vajroli, Shakti Chalana.

    The 5 groups — full examples

    • Hasta (hand): Jnana / Chin, Prana, Apana, Vayu, Shunya, Prithvi, Varuna, Surya.
    • Mana (head): Shambhavi, Nasikagra Drishti, Khechari, Shanmukhi.
    • Kaya (body): Viparita Karani, Yoga Mudra, Pashini, Tadagi, Maha Mudra.
    • Adhara (pelvic): Ashwini, Vajroli.
    • Bandha (lock): Mula, Uddiyana, Jalandhara, Maha Bandha (all three).

    This 5-group system is from Swami Satyananda's Asana Pranayama Mudra Bandha (Bihar School), a reference book in your syllabus.

    The locks

    BandhaMethodEffectAvoid if
    Jalandhara (throat)Chin pressed to the chest during breath-holdingPressure near the carotid sinus → heart rate ↓, calm mind. Stops prana going up.Neck problems, low BP
    Uddiyana (belly)After a full out-breath, pull the belly in and upMassages belly organs, helps digestion. Prana moves up.Ulcer, hernia, heart disease, pregnancy
    Mula (root)Contract the perineum / pelvic floorPelvic-floor strength. Holds Apana. Helps bladder control (continence).Pelvic infection, pregnancy (strong form)
    Maha BandhaAll three togetherCombined effect. HYP 3.19–25.All the above

    The neuro-pranic mechanism — new in 2026

    • Hands → brain: the hands and fingers have a very large area in the brain's sensory and motor map (homunculus). A hand mudra sends a steady signal to a big part of the brain. This helps concentration.
    • Pressure → nerves: bandhas change the pressure in the neck, belly and pelvis. Jalandhara presses near the pressure sensors (baroreceptors) in the neck, so the heart slows.
    • Prana language: mudras close the "leaks" of prana and send it into Sushumna. So the mind is ready for meditation.

    Research

    Nataesh 2025 (randomised crossover, 30 healthy people): 10 minutes after Uddiyana Bandha, vagal (calming) activity was dominant. Safety: diastolic BP rose right after the bandha.

    Exam note: the brain-map and pressure ideas are scientific ideas, but direct research on mudras is still small. Write "may help" for vitality and stress.

      Turning the senses inward (Pratyahara)

      SyllabusPratyahara (Withdrawal of Senses): Applications in overcoming sensory overload, digital addiction, and emotional reactivity

      The senses stop running after things. They turn back to the mind.

      Think of it like thisYou switch off your phone alerts so you can read in peace. Pratyahara switches off the pull of the senses.
      Remember
      1. YS 2.54: the senses do not touch their objects. They follow the mind.
      2. YS 2.55: from it comes full control of the senses (Parama Vashyata).
      3. It helps: anxiety, too much input, phone addiction, quick reactions, craving.
      4. Practices: Yoga Nidra, Shanmukhi Mudra, silence (Mauna), digital fasting.
      For the exam — what to write
      svaviṣayāsamprayoge cittasvarūpānukāra ivendriyāṇāṃ pratyāhāraḥ — Yoga Sutra 2.54

      Problem → how it helps → practice

      ProblemHow Pratyahara helpsPractice
      AnxietyLess input to the senses → fewer alarm signals → the mind settlesYoga Nidra, Shanmukhi Mudra, breath awareness
      Sensory overload (new in 2026)Gives the senses rest, and a choice of what to take inSilence (Mauna), eyes-closed practice, time in nature
      Digital addiction (new in 2026)Breaks the automatic pull of screens. Builds the "pause" before you react.Digital fasting, fixed screen hours, Trataka instead of scrolling
      Emotional reactivity (new in 2026)You watch the sense and the feeling before you act on itInner silence (Antar Mauna), witnessing
      Sensual desiresThe senses stop running after objects, so craving (Raga) becomes thinPratyahara + Vairagya + Satsang

      Research

      No trial tests Pratyahara alone. These studies test yoga or mindfulness, which include it.

      • Cramer 2018 (meta-analysis, 8 RCTs, n = 319): yoga reduced anxiety compared with no treatment (SMD −0.43). It showed no effect in diagnosed anxiety disorders.
      • Telles 2009 (Patanjali Research Foundation, n = 300): one 2-hour yoga practice session cut state anxiety by 14.7%. A yoga theory session cut it by only 3.4%.
      • Fendel 2024 (meta-analysis, 19 RCTs + 20 other trials): mindfulness programmes reduced problem internet use (g = −1.67 in the RCTs). Evidence quality is low.
      • Rao 2026 (S-VYASA, RCT, 120 school students): 8 weeks of yoga reduced gaming-disorder symptoms, internet addiction and stress.

      Answer plan — anxiety through Pratyahara

      1. YS 2.54–55 + the turtle drawing.
      2. What anxiety is: sense → alarm → worry loop.
      3. The table: problem → how → practice.
      4. Practices: Yoga Nidra, Shanmukhi, Antar Mauna.
      5. One research line: Cramer 2018 or Telles 2009.

      Concentration (Dharana)

      SyllabusDharana (Concentration): One-pointed focus techniques: mantra, breath, chakra, and trataka, Improvement of attention span, executive functioning, and cognitive clarity

      Dharana is holding the mind on one place or object (YS 3.1).

      Think of it like thisYou hold a torch beam on one spot on the wall. When your hand drifts, you bring it back.
      Focus onExample
      MantraOm, Gayatri
      BreathCounting breaths, the nostril tip
      ChakraThe heart, the eyebrow centre
      TratakaA flame, a dot
      Remember
      1. 4 focus tools: mantra, breath, chakra, Trataka.
      2. Gains: longer attention, better planning and self-control, clearer thinking.
      3. The mind wanders less.
      For the exam — what to write
      deśabandhaścittasya dhāraṇā — Yoga Sutra 3.1

      Dharana = binding the mind to one place or object.

      Benefits

      Longer attention span · better executive function (planning, self-control, working memory) · clearer thinking · less mind-wandering.

      Research

      • Talwadkar 2014 (S-VYASA, RCT, 60 elderly people, 26 days): the Trataka group did better than the wait-list group on letter cancellation and Trail Making B (attention tests).

      Unit 2 · Point 3 of 5Must learn

      Meditation (Dhyana)

      SyllabusDhyana (Meditation): Stages of meditation, difference between Dharana and Dhyana, Mindfulness, open awareness, effortless meditation, Benefits for emotional stability, neuroplasticity, compassion, resilience

      In Dhyana the mind flows to one object without a break (YS 3.2).

      Think of it like thisDharana is like water dripping: drip, drip. Dhyana is like oil poured from a pot: one smooth stream.
      Pratyahara — the senses turn in
      ↓
      Dharana — fix the mind (YS 3.1)
      ↓
      Dhyana — the mind flows (YS 3.2)
      ↓
      Samadhi — only the object shines (YS 3.3)
      Remember
      1. Dharana = effort, the mind slips and comes back. Dhyana = little effort, a steady flow.
      2. Dharana + Dhyana + Samadhi on one object = Samyama (YS 3.4).
      3. 3 modern kinds: focused, open (mindfulness), effortless.
      4. Gains: steady feelings, a brain that changes (neuroplasticity), compassion, resilience.
      For the exam — what to write
      tatra pratyayaikatānatā dhyānam — Yoga Sutra 3.2

      Dharana

      • Effort to hold the mind
      • The mind slips and is brought back
      • Like drops of water: drip, drip
      • Stage of training

      Dhyana

      • Little or no effort
      • Continuous flow
      • Like oil poured from a pot: one stream
      • Stage of result

      Modern kinds of meditation

      Modern kindWhat you doYoga example
      Focused attentionHold one objectTrataka, mantra japa
      Mindfulness / open awarenessWatch whatever comes, without judgingWitnessing (Sakshi Bhava), Vipassana
      EffortlessLet the mind settle by itselfAjapa japa, Om meditation

      Benefits

      Emotional stability · neuroplasticity (the brain changes its structure with practice) · more compassion (Unit 4 · Point 3) · resilience.

      Research

      • Lazar 2005 (20 long-term meditators vs controls): meditators had a thicker prefrontal cortex and right anterior insula.
      • Hölzel 2011 (16 vs 17 people): 8 weeks of mindfulness (MBSR) increased grey matter in the left hippocampus, posterior cingulate and temporo-parietal junction. This is neuroplasticity.
      • Fox 2014 (meta-analysis, 21 studies): 8 brain regions differ in meditators (d = 0.46). The authors warn of publication bias.
      • Aggression: Deshpande 2008 (RCT, n = 226, 8 weeks): integrated yoga reduced verbal aggressiveness more than exercise (P = 0.013). Bilderbeck 2013 (RCT, 100 prisoners, 10 weeks): less stress and distress, and better response inhibition.

      Short answer — "Difference between Dharana and Dhyana. Stages of meditation": the two lists + the 4-step flow + Samyama.

      Full absorption (Samadhi)

      SyllabusSamadhi (Absorption): Conceptual understanding, levels, and relevance in spiritual psychology, Samadhi as an outcome of integrated Hatha-Raja Yoga practice

      In Samadhi only the object shines. You forget "I am meditating" (YS 3.3).

      Think of it like thisYou read a great story and forget you are reading. Only the story is there.
      With support (Samprajnata) — gross object → subtle object → bliss → pure I-sense (YS 1.17)
      ↓
      Without support (Asamprajnata) — only the hidden impressions stay (YS 1.18)
      ↓
      With seed → without seed (Sabija → Nirbija) — the last stage before freedom (Kaivalya) (YS 1.46, 1.51)
      Remember
      1. Two levels: with support (Samprajnata) and without support (Asamprajnata).
      2. HYP 1.1–2: Hatha Yoga is a ladder to Raja Yoga.
      3. So Samadhi is the result of Hatha + Raja practice together.
      4. In psychology it is like Maslow's "peak experience".
      For the exam — what to write
      tadevārthamātranirbhāsaṃ svarūpaśūnyamiva samādhiḥ — Yoga Sutra 3.3

      In Samadhi only the object shines. The mind seems empty of itself.

      LevelMeaningSutra
      Samprajnata (with support)Four steps: Vitarka (gross object) → Vichara (subtle object) → Ananda (bliss) → Asmita (pure I-sense)YS 1.17
      Asamprajnata (without support)Only the latent impressions (Samskaras) stayYS 1.18
      Sabija → NirbijaWith seed → without seed. The last stage before Kaivalya.YS 1.46, 1.51

      The Hatha–Raja link

      HYP 1.1–2: Hatha Yoga is a ladder to Raja Yoga. HYP 4.3–4 gives other names for Samadhi: Raja Yoga, Unmani, Manonmani, Laya, Sahaja, Turiya and more. So Samadhi is the outcome of integrated Hatha + Raja practice.

      Spiritual psychology

      Samadhi is like "peak experience" and "self-transcendence" in Maslow's later work. It gives meaning, peace and freedom from the ego.

      Rules for others and for yourself (Yama and Niyama)

      SyllabusIntegration of Yamas and Niyamas: Ethical foundations for mental purification, Role in psychological hygiene, interpersonal relationships, stress resilience, Application in daily lifestyle, counseling, and self-management

      Yamas guide how you treat others. Niyamas guide how you treat yourself.

      Think of it like thisYamas are the traffic rules: how you drive among other people. Niyamas are servicing your own car.
