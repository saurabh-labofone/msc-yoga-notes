Unit 1 of 6 · 10 hours

Vyasa explains the Yoga Sutra

Patanjali wrote yoga in very short lines. Vyasa wrote the first full explanation. Every later teacher explains Vyasa.

Syllabus title: व्यास भाष्य पर आधारित योग दर्शन (Yoga philosophy based on the Vyasa Bhashya)

    Unit 1 · Point 1 of 5Must learn

    Who was Vyasa?

    Syllabusव्यासाचार्य का परिचय एवं ऐतिहासिक पृष्ठभूमि — Vyasa: introduction and background

    Vyasa wrote the oldest full explanation of the Yoga Sutra. It is called the Yoga Bhashya.

    Think of it like thisPatanjali's Yoga Sutra is like a list of very short text messages. Vyasa is the teacher who sits beside it and explains each message.
    Remember
    1. His book: the Yoga Bhashya (explanation).
    2. His time: about 400 CE. Scholars do not agree on the exact date.
    3. It is the oldest explanation we have. All later teachers explain Vyasa's words.
    4. His famous line: "Yoga is Samadhi" (Yogah Samadhih).
    For the exam — what to write

    Who

    Tradition says Vyasa is Veda Vyasa (Krishna Dvaipayana), who also wrote the Mahabharata. Most scholars think he is a later teacher who used this respected name. Some modern scholars (Philipp Maas) think the Sutra and the Bhashya are one work, the "Patanjala Yogashastra".

    Why he matters

    • First and most trusted explanation.
    • He gives the full system: chitta, gunas, kleshas, karma, Ishvara.
    • He uses pictures: the river of the mind (YS 1.12), the burnt seed (YS 2.4), the four parts of medicine (YS 2.15).
    • He quotes the older Samkhya teacher Panchashikha.
    • His chapter endings call the work "Samkhya-pravachana" — an explanation of Samkhya.
    yogaḥ samādhiḥ — Vyasa on Yoga Sutra 1.1

    Answer plan

    1. Draw the timeline (in "Start here").
    2. Who · when · what.
    3. Why he matters (5 points).
    4. "Yoga is Samadhi" + meaning.

    Unit 1 · Point 3 of 5Must learn

    Mind, waves and stillness — Chitta, Vritti, Nirodha

    Syllabusचित्त, वृत्ति एवं निरोध की व्यासीय व्याख्या — Vyasa's explanation of Chitta, Vritti and Nirodha

    Yoga means the waves of the mind become still (YS 1.2).

    Think of it like thisThe mind (Chitta) is a lake. Thoughts (Vritti) are waves. When the waves stop (Nirodha), you can see the bottom of the lake clearly. That clear seeing is yoga.
    Remember
    1. Chitta = the whole mind. It is made of the 3 gunas.
    2. 5 waves (Vrittis): right knowledge, wrong knowledge, imagination with words, sleep, memory.
    3. 5 levels of mind (Bhumis): restless, dull, distracted, one-pointed, stopped. Vyasa: only the last two are yoga.
    4. Vyasa's picture: the mind is a river that can flow two ways — to good or to harm.
    For the exam — what to write
    yogaś cittavṛttinirodhaḥ — Yoga Sutra 1.2

    Vyasa's points

    • The mind has 3 tendencies: clarity (Prakhya, Sattva), activity (Pravritti, Rajas), inertia (Sthiti, Tamas).
    • The mind is not conscious by itself. Like a magnet, it serves the self (Purusha) only by being near it (on YS 1.4).
    • Each vritti can be painful (Klishta) or not painful (Aklishta).
    • In Nirodha, the waves stop and only deep impressions (Samskaras) remain (YS 1.18).

    The 5 levels of mind — Vyasa on YS 1.1

    LevelLikeYoga?

    Vikshipta — distractedCalm for a moment, then lostNo
    Ekagra — one-pointedA still flameYes — samadhi with an object

    The 5 vrittis (YS 1.6)

    Pramana (right knowledge: seeing, inference, trusted word) · Viparyaya (wrong knowledge) · Vikalpa (words with no real object, like "the horn of a hare") · Nidra (sleep) · Smriti (memory).

    Unit 1 · Point 4 of 5Must learn

    The 5 troubles (Kleshas) and the store of karma (Karmashaya)

    Syllabusक्लेश सिद्धांत एवं कर्माशय की अवधारणा — Klesha theory and the idea of Karmashaya

    Five troubles in the mind make us act. Our actions are stored, and later they give fruit.

    Think of it like thisA klesha is a seed. When we act on it, it grows into a plant (karma). The plant gives fruit: our birth, how long we live, and our happy and sad times. A burnt seed can never grow again.
    Seed — Klesha (the trouble)
    ↓
    Plant — Karma (the action, stored as Karmashaya)
    ↓
    Fruit — birth (Jati) · life length (Ayu) · experience (Bhoga) — YS 2.13
    Remember
    1. The 5 Kleshas: not-knowing (Avidya), "I-am" feeling (Asmita), liking (Raga), disliking (Dvesha), fear of death (Abhinivesha).
    2. Avidya is the field where the other four grow.
    3. Vyasa: wisdom burns the klesha like a roasted seed. Then no new fruit comes.
    For the exam — what to write
    avidyāsmitārāgadveṣābhiniveśāḥ kleśāḥ — Yoga Sutra 2.3
    KleshaSutraMeaning
    Avidya2.5Taking the temporary as lasting, impure as pure, pain as pleasure, non-self as self
    Asmita2.6Mixing up the seer with the instrument of seeing
    Raga2.7Pull toward past pleasure
    Dvesha2.8Push away from past pain
    Abhinivesha2.9Fear of death, even in the wise. Vyasa: even a newborn creature has it — a sign of past lives.

    4 states of a klesha (YS 2.4) + Vyasa's 5th

    Prasupta (asleep) → Tanu (thin) → Vicchinna (hidden for a time by another klesha) → Udara (fully active). Vyasa adds a fifth state: Dagdha-bija (burnt seed), burnt by discriminative knowledge (Prasankhyana).

    How to remove them

    1. Kriya Yoga makes them thin (2.2).
    2. Meditation removes their active waves (2.11).
    3. The subtle seeds go back into their source (Pratiprasava, 2.10).

    Karmashaya

    kleśamūlaḥ karmāśayo dṛṣṭādṛṣṭajanmavedanīyaḥ — Yoga Sutra 2.12
    • Fruit comes in this life (seen) or a later life (unseen) — 2.12.
    • Fruit = birth, life length, experience — 2.13. Pleasure or pain, from good or bad deeds — 2.14.
    • Vyasa: the karmashaya is mostly "Ekabhavika" — the karma of one life ripens together as the next birth.
    • Karmashaya vs Vasana: Karmashaya gives birth, life span, experience. Vasanas are deep habits that show up when a matching birth comes (4.8–4.10).

    4 kinds of karma (YS 4.7)

    Black (harmful) · White-black (mixed, most people) · White (tapas, study, meditation) · Neither (the yogi, who wants no fruit).

    Kriya Yoga — 3 daily habits

    Linked toPoint 4 — it is the remedy for the kleshas

    Kriya Yoga is three daily habits that make the troubles (Kleshas) weak (YS 2.1–2.2).

    Think of it like thisBrushing your teeth every day keeps cavities small. Kriya Yoga every day keeps the kleshas small.
    Remember
    1. Tapas — self-discipline. Vyasa: it must not disturb the calm of the mind.
    2. Svadhyaya — study of wisdom books and repeating Om.
    3. Ishvara Pranidhana — offer all your actions to God.
    4. Two aims: build samadhi, and make the kleshas thin.
    For the exam — what to write
    tapaḥsvādhyāyeśvarapraṇidhānāni kriyāyogaḥ — Yoga Sutra 2.1

    Fruits: tapas → perfection of body and senses (2.43) · svadhyaya → contact with your chosen deity (2.44) · Ishvara Pranidhana → samadhi (2.45).

    Kriya Yoga is for the beginner whose mind is still distracted.

    Unit 1 · Point 5 of 5Must learn

    Samkhya and Yoga — the map and the walk

    Syllabusसांख्य–योग दर्शन का समन्वय — The joining of Samkhya and Yoga

    Samkhya gives the map of the world. Yoga shows how to walk on it.

    Think of it like thisYou watch a film. You, the watcher, never change. The film always changes. Trouble starts when you forget you are the watcher. The watcher is Purusha. The film is Prakriti.
    Remember
    1. Both say: there is a watcher (Purusha) and nature (Prakriti) with 3 gunas.
    2. Both say: freedom comes when you see the difference clearly (Viveka).
    3. Yoga adds God (Ishvara). So Yoga is called "Samkhya with Ishvara" (Seshvara Samkhya).
    4. Samkhya stresses knowing. Yoga stresses practice.
    For the exam — what to write

    Same in both

    • Purusha and Prakriti
    • Many Purushas
    • 3 gunas, 24 products of Prakriti
    • The effect exists in the cause (Satkaryavada)
    • Suffering = the seer and the seen look joined
    • Goal = Kaivalya, by Viveka

    Different

    • Ishvara: Samkhya does not need it. Yoga accepts it (1.23–1.29).
    • Method: knowledge vs practice (Ashtanga, Abhyasa).
    • Mind: Yoga uses one word, Chitta, for Buddhi + Ahamkara + Manas.
    • Psychology: Yoga adds vrittis, kleshas, samadhi levels, obstacles.

    Yoga's own words for the levels (YS 2.19)

    Specific (Vishesha) 16: 5 gross elements + 11 organs · Non-specific (Avishesha) 6: 5 subtle elements + I-sense · Mere sign (Linga-matra) 1: Mahat · No sign (Alinga) 1: Prakriti.

    Vyasa's chapter endings call his book "Samkhya-pravachana" — this shows how close the two are.

    Asked before: Prakriti and Purusha · 25 elements of Samkhya — Dec 2023, Jan–Feb 2022, Dec 2023 (Hindi).

    Vachaspati explains Vyasa

    Vyasa's words were short and deep. About 500 years later, Vachaspati Mishra explained every line of Vyasa, so every student could follow it.

    Syllabus title: वाचस्पति मिश्र – तत्त्व वैशारदी (Vachaspati Mishra — Tattva Vaisharadi)

      Unit 2 · Point 1 of 5Must learn

      Who was Vachaspati Mishra?

      Syllabusवाचस्पति मिश्र का दार्शनिक योगदान — His contribution to philosophy

      Vachaspati was a scholar who wrote clear books on almost every school of Indian philosophy.

      Think of it like thisA teacher who can teach maths, science, history and language — and teaches each one very well, in its own way.
      Remember
      1. From Mithila (north Bihar). 9th or 10th century.
      2. Title: "Sarva-tantra-svatantra" — independent master of all systems.
      3. His yoga book: Tattva Vaisharadi.
      4. His Samkhya book: Samkhya Tattva Kaumudi. His Vedanta book: Bhamati.
      5. In all he wrote 7 works. One of them, the Tattva-samiksha, is lost.

      Date: one of his books gives the year 898 of an era. Scholars read it as 841 CE or 976 CE.

      His gift: he explained each school from the inside, in its own terms. So his Samkhya and Yoga books became the standard reading.

      What kind of book is the Tattva Vaisharadi?

      Syllabusतत्त्व वैशारदी का स्वरूप (व्यास भाष्य पर टीका) — Its nature: notes on Vyasa's Bhashya

      It is an explanation of Vyasa's explanation. This kind of book is called a Tika.

      Think of it like thisPatanjali wrote the question. Vyasa wrote the answer. Vachaspati wrote clear notes on the answer, so every student can follow it.
      Read the Sutra (Patanjali)
      ↓
      Read Vyasa's line
      ↓
      Vachaspati's notes: word meanings · the hidden link · a question and its answer · an example
      Remember
      1. Name means "skill in the truth".
      2. Faithful — he follows Vyasa and rarely disagrees.
      3. Exact — clear meanings of hard words.
      4. Logical — he answers objections from other schools.
      5. Fills gaps — he says what Vyasa only hints at.
      For the exam — what to write

      Method: first the objection (Purvapaksha), then the answer (Siddhanta) — the debate style of Nyaya.

      Example of "fills gaps": he says what each type of samadhi takes as its object (see Point 3).

      He reads Yoga with the same Samkhya map as his Samkhya Tattva Kaumudi.

      Samadhi and truth-bearing wisdom (Ritambhara Prajna)

      Syllabusसमाधि, प्रज्ञा एवं ऋतम्भरा प्रज्ञा की व्याख्या — Samadhi, Prajna and Ritambhara Prajna

      Samadhi has steps. At a high step, a wisdom appears that holds only truth.

      Think of it like thisReading about a mango gives you general knowledge. Guessing its taste gives general knowledge too. Tasting the mango gives true, direct knowledge. Ritambhara Prajna is the tasting.

      Remember
      1. Two kinds: samadhi with an object (Samprajnata, "with seed") and without an object (Asamprajnata, "seedless").
      2. Samadhi with an object has 4 types: Vitarka, Vichara, Ananda, Asmita (YS 1.17).
      3. Vyasa says Ritambhara "bears only truth", with no trace of error. Vachaspati adds nothing here. He only says the Bhashya is easy.

      Unit 3 of 6 · 10 hours

      Vijnanabhikshu checks them both

      About 600 years after Vachaspati, Vijnanabhikshu read Vyasa again. He questioned the older notes and said that Samkhya, Yoga and Vedanta teach one truth.

      Syllabus title: विज्ञान भिक्षु – योग वार्तिक (Vijnanabhikshu — Yoga Vartika)

        Unit 3 · Point 1 of 5Must learn

        Who was Vijnanabhikshu?

        Syllabusविज्ञान भिक्षु का परिचय एवं दार्शनिक दृष्टिकोण — Introduction and his view

        A 16th-century teacher who said: Samkhya, Yoga and Vedanta all teach one truth.

        Think of it like thisOne garden, three windows. Each window shows a different part of the same garden. Samkhya, Yoga and Vedanta are three windows.
        Remember
        1. Time: about the 16th century.
        2. His yoga books: Yoga Vartika (on Vyasa) and Yoga Sara Sangraha (a short summary).
        3. He also wrote on Samkhya (Samkhya Pravachana Bhashya) and Vedanta (Vijnanamrita Bhashya).
        4. He puts Vedanta at the top and gives God (Ishvara) a bigger place.
        For the exam — what to write

        His main view

        • All schools agree. Read correctly, Samkhya, Yoga and Vedanta teach the same truth.
        • Vedanta is the top. Brahman is the highest. Samkhya and Yoga are steps toward it.
        • Against "the world is only illusion" (Mayavada) of Shankara's later followers.
        • Theistic. Ishvara has a larger role than in Samkhya.

        Other book: Samkhya Sara.

        Three schools explain God and the soul differently. Vijnanabhikshu joined their best ideas.

        Think of it like thisA wave and the ocean. The wave is different from the ocean, but it is never separate from it. That is his idea: "not divided" (Avibhaga).

            Modern view and research

            In 1896 Vivekananda explained Patanjali to the modern world. Today scientists test yoga in labs. This unit also shows how you write a research paper about an old text.

            Syllabus title: आधुनिक भाष्य परंपरा एवं शोध अनुप्रयोग (The modern commentary tradition and research use)

              Unit 6 · Point 1 of 5Must learn

              Vivekananda's Raja Yoga

              Syllabusस्वामी विवेकानन्द का राज योग – भाष्यात्मक दृष्टि — Vivekananda's Raja Yoga as a commentary

              In 1896 Vivekananda explained Patanjali's yoga as a science that anyone can test.

              Think of it like thisA lab manual. Do not just believe. Do the steps, and see the result yourself.
              Remember
              1. Book: Raja Yoga, 1896, from his talks in New York.
              2. Part 1: talks (prana, concentration, meditation). Part 2: his translation of the Yoga Sutra with short notes.
              3. Famous line, at the start of the book: "Each soul is potentially divine."
              4. He started the modern world-wide interest in Patanjali.
              For the exam — what to write
              • Science: he presents Raja Yoga as a practical method, to be tested by experience.
              • For everyone: not tied to one religion. Written for the West and modern India.
              • Body words of his time: he compares Ida and Pingala to nerve currents and Sushumna to the canal in the spinal cord.
              • Concentration first: all the power of the mind comes from concentration.
              • Vedanta reading: he reads Purusha as the Atman of Vedanta.

              Old explanation vs modern science

              Syllabusपारंपरिक भाष्य बनाम आधुनिक वैज्ञानिक व्याख्या — Traditional commentary vs modern scientific explanation

              The old explanation asks "What does it mean?". Science asks "Can we measure it?".

              Think of it like thisA map and a GPS. The map shows the whole land and its meaning. The GPS checks exactly where you are, one point at a time. You need both.

              TrustsThe text and the teacherData from tests
              MethodReasoning, debate, inner experienceMeasure, compare groups, statistics
              GoalFreedom (Kaivalya)Health, well-being
              Prana isLife force in the NadisBreath, nervous system, heart rhythm

              1. Vivekananda (1896) — yoga as a science.
              2. Swami Kuvalayananda — Kaivalyadhama and the journal Yoga Mimamsa (1924) — first lab studies of yoga.
              3. Today — S-VYASA, Patanjali Research Foundation, trials on PubMed.

              3. Give the road to science: Vivekananda 1896 → Kuvalayananda and Yoga Mimamsa 1924 → today's research centres.

              How to write research on an old commentary

              Syllabusभाष्य आधारित शोध लेखन की प्रक्रिया — The process of commentary-based research writing

              Pick one idea, read every teacher on it, compare them, link to today, and write with exact references.

              Think of it like thisCooking from many recipes for one dish: collect the recipes, compare them, cook your own version, and say where each idea came from.
              1 · Pick one small topic — for example, Ishvara in YS 1.23
              ↓
              2 · Collect the texts — the sutra and every commentary; note the edition
              ↓
              3 · Read in order — Sutra → Bhashya → Tika → Vartika → modern
              ↓
              4 · Make a comparison table — teachers × ideas: agree, differ, missing
              ↓
              5 · Link to today — psychology, therapy, research papers
              ↓
              6 · Say your main point — one clear argument
              ↓
              7 · Write and cite — text + verse + teacher + translator, for example "Vyasa on YS 2.15 (tr. Woods 1914)"
              Common mistakes
              1. Using only one translation.
              2. Mixing up the sutra and the commentary.
              3. No verse numbers.
              4. Saying science "proves" a text.
              For the exam — what to write

              Answer plan

              1. List the 7 steps in order.
              2. Give one example topic: Ishvara in YS 1.23.
              3. Show a small comparison table: teachers × ideas.
              4. Show one correct reference, for example "Vyasa on YS 2.15 (tr. Woods 1914)".
              5. End with the 4 common mistakes.
              Unit 6 · Point 5 of 5Must learn

              Two kinds of paper: concept paper and review paper

              Syllabusवैचारिक शोध-पत्र एवं समीक्षा आलेख लेखन — Writing a conceptual paper and a review article

              A concept paper builds a new idea. A review paper collects what other studies found.

              Think of it like thisA concept paper is like designing a new house. A review paper is like visiting every house on a street and writing a report.

              Concept paper

              No new data. Joins ideas into a new framework. Four designs (Jaakkola 2020): theory synthesis, theory adaptation, typology, model.

              Review paper

              Sums up existing studies on one question. Types: narrative, systematic, scoping, meta-analysis. Grant and Booth (2009) list 14 types.
              Remember
              1. Concept paper: no new data, a new framework.
              2. Review paper: sums up other studies on one question.
              3. Jaakkola 2020: 4 designs of a concept paper.
              4. PRISMA 2020: a 27-item checklist and a flow diagram for systematic reviews.
              For the exam — what to write

              Parts of a concept paper

              Title · Abstract · Introduction (the problem) · Concepts and sources · Your argument or model · Discussion · Uses · Conclusion · References.

              Parts of a review paper

              Title · Abstract · Introduction (gap, aim) · Methods (databases, search words, include and exclude rules) · Results (table of studies, PRISMA flow) · Discussion · Limits · Conclusion.

              PRISMA 2020 (Page et al. 2021, BMJ): the guide for systematic reviews — a 27-item checklist and a flow diagram (found → screened → checked → included). See also Paper 106.
